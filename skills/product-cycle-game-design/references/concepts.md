# Game concept patterns

These are Product Cycle's reusable proposal patterns, not an official OpenAI concept catalog. Adapt to the accepted goal, skills, device and session context. Start from player actions and world consequences; visual genre and art style follow the selected play concept. Do not copy characters/assets from reference games or silently add unsupported capabilities.

| Pattern | Player agency and loop | Presentation that conveys play | Smallest useful play trial | Educational fit and risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typing / target defense | Read a cue, prioritize/select a target, type to fire, resolve threats, decide next target. Adjust pace or resources only if consistent with accepted difficulty. | Arena with moving or turn-based threats, readable target hierarchy, firing/impact and changing world state; HUD supports the arena. | Multiple distinguishable targets, target choice, hit/miss/recovery and a complete restartable encounter. | Retrieval can drive attacks. Visible answer copying tests typing instead; crowded targets and speed can compete with reading. |
| Puzzle / construction | Inspect constraints, place/connect/manipulate pieces, see a changed configuration, revise until a goal is met. | Manipulable board/world with clear affordances and consequences, rather than answer buttons alone. | One puzzle that permits meaningful choices and a recoverable mistake, with another configuration to try. | Language matching or sentence building can constrain moves. Avoid implying that solving one puzzle proves mastery. |
| Exploration / adventure | Navigate, discover clues, decide where/how to interact, unlock a consequence and explore further. | Navigable scene/map, focal objects and character presence where appropriate; dialogue supports the world. | One location, an interaction with an effect and a choice that changes what is accessible. | Contextual vocabulary can support discovery; exploration may distract from the intended retrieval practice. |
| Platform / movement challenge | Move/jump/dodge, read geometry and hazards, attempt a route, recover and refine timing. | Camera/world space, character and terrain; grounded movement, collision and goal feedback. | One route with a meaningful obstacle, responsive control, recovery and reachable goal. | Use only when movement is part of the intended experience; simultaneous typing and precise movement may conflict. |
| Rhythm / timing | Read a cue, choose/perform a timed action, perceive synchronization, adjust on the next beat. | Timeline or spatial timing cues tied to actual motion; audio only when supported and in scope. | A short repeatable sequence with early/late/correct responses and controllable pace. | May suit pronunciation/rhythm. Tight timing can penalize language processing or accessibility; offer the approved alternative. |
| Turn-based tactics / encounters | Read a situation, choose a constrained action, resolve its effects, respond to the new state. | Board/encounter scene, readable units/resources and clear action resolution. | One encounter with consequential choices, interpretable opponent/system response and recovery/replay. | Language actions may power chosen tactics; avoid bolting on a quiz that merely gates unrelated combat. |

## Concept card

For each serious alternative, record a short playable scenario rather than a feature wishlist:

- Player role, chosen genre and intended experience; preserve owner constraints.
- Moment-to-moment verbs, decision and consequence; show an opening and a second attempt.
- Core/session loop; challenge or uncertainty, recovery, feedback and reason to continue. Progression is optional and must support the chosen game.
- Gameplay composition: world/arena, actor/targets, HUD, controls, animation/art needs and how the primary action is visible.
- Why it fits the product outcome; likely friction, scope/capability costs and the riskiest assumption.
- Small play trial that could disprove the concept, planned observation and owner decision still needed. Identify illustrative parameters as tuning hypotheses.

When genre is fixed, compare variants inside it. For a requested vocabulary shooter, plausible proposals include target-priority typing defense, a paced aiming/recall encounter, or tactical turn-based word combat. Choose from owner intent and device, not presumed superiority. Keep the same learning goal when comparing mechanics. A calm shooter can be deliberate and low pressure; it still needs recognizable firing/target consequences, not just a decorative gun beside an input.

Do not treat this catalog as mandatory features, research findings or a choice already approved by the owner.
